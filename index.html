How to play

The rules of the land

Your people farm, settle and fight on their own. You decide what they discover, where they go, and who they call a friend.

  1. Discover. When science fills the bar, pick a tech. Techs unlock other techs, upgrade your soldiers, and each new era opens only when you research its keystone.
  2. Shape your draft. On the level-up screen you can Reroll the three cards (3 per game, plus 1 each new era), Banish a troop line or civic so it never appears again this game (3 per game), or Skip for 40 influence. Keys: R, B and K.
  3. Level up. Science is your experience bar. Each level, pick one of three cards: military, science or expansion. Every troop is a line that modernizes as it levels: Lv 1 is its Ancient form, Lv 8 its Future form (Spearmen → Phalanx → Pikemen … → Shield Mechs). A troop can be at most two ages ahead of your era, and the Future form also needs Astronomy 3, so science and growth decide how modern your army gets. When a new era begins, troops left behind modernize for free. Levels 3, 6 and 8 also change how a troop fights, and level 4 unlocks its special attack. Some troop lines hide evolutions and unions. Recipes are secret until you discover them: when your army stumbles onto one, its slot glows “Something stirs”, and the next War Chest (from a rival hero or a Warlord, marked with a chest) reveals it. Discoveries are kept forever in the Collection, which also holds clues. Each rival nation guards one legendary union and fields it late in the game: defeat that nation to learn it. New troops unlock as you achieve things.
  4. Lead your hero. Every people has a hero you steer yourself, and the camera follows them. Tap the map to march there, or tap an enemy city to attack it. WASD steers too. Any of your troops that step inside your hero’s circle join the band, with no limit: they follow and fight with you until they die. Troops busy defending their own city stay put. Dragging looks around the map, and H brings the camera back. G releases your band to guard the nearest city. Space fires the hero’s ability. A fallen hero returns to the capital after 60 seconds. Rival nations have heroes too, and slaying one earns a War Chest. P pauses.
  5. Explore the wilds. The map is hidden until your hero, troops or borders see it. Out in the unclaimed land you’ll find ancient ruins, barbarian camps (clear the guards, then walk onto the loot), mercenary camps, lost tribes that join your band, shrines that bless it, and three natural wonders that boost your empire once they sit inside your borders. Ruins and camps hide artifacts: powers your hero keeps all game, up to six at a time. New sites keep appearing. A site swallowed by a rival’s borders is lost.
  6. Build. Food grows your cities. Production decides how many troops you field, up to 90 army room: big troops take more room (a Titan takes about 4 times what a Spear Line does). Siege and Sky break city walls fastest, but any army can wear a city down. Influence pushes your borders and pays for diplomacy. Buildings rise on their own as the eras pass.
  7. Deal. Six rival peoples share the land, each with its own color, hero and guarded legend, and everyone starts somewhere random. Borders are closed: armies can only enter a nation’s land if they are at war with it or allied, so wars are fought between neighbors or around the edges. Spend influence on gifts, trade and alliances, or go to war and take their cities. All the influence you earn also pushes your borders: more of it lets each city claim more land.
  8. Counters. Every troop hunts with its main type and deals 75% more damage to its prey: Line beats Mounted and Titans, Mounted beats Ranged, Siege and Fire, Ranged beats Line, Swarm and flyers, Titans beat Swarm and Mounted, Swarm beats Siege and Fire, Siege beats Titans and Line, Fire beats Ranged and Support. Crossbows also pierce Titans. Rivals watch your army and raise its counters, so mix your troops. Each card shows what it’s strong and weak against.
  9. Win. Conquer all six rival nations, by war or by diplomatic union, or research the Space Program and launch a starship. The starship is a long siege: pick three launch sites (big cities build faster, frontline ones are exposed), survive at least 8 minutes of building while your neighbors and then the whole world come for those sites, choose a defense protocol at the start and at each third, then hold every site through a 3-minute launch. Take four nations and the rest also unite against you. Lose your capital, or let a rival launch first, and it’s over.
  10. Morale. Rival nations lose heart when they lose cities, generals and battles. Below a third, their wounded troops rout and run home, their soldiers desert as their cities fall, and a broken nation may offer to surrender. When a rival host gathers and marches on one of your cities, you get a warning with its size.
  11. Climb the Crowns. Chieftain is for learning. King is Crown 0. Win it to open Crown 1, and each Crown adds one twist and pays more Glory, up to Crown 10. Every win also earns a grade, from Bronze to Star, for winning fast, losing no city, keeping your general alive and bringing every nation down.
  12. Core and supports. Your people’s starting troop is your core: it costs 30% less to raise and keep, reaches Lv 8, and gets +15% health and damage when it is half your army, +30% at three quarters, but rivals counter it harder. A support slot opens when the Classical Age dawns and a second in the Medieval Age: each time, you choose from three lines drawn from everything you have unlocked from the ages you have reached. Supports top out at Lv 7, but a union of two lines can reach Lv 8, and it frees a slot. Use Reroll and Banish to fish for the supports you want.
  13. Taking a capital. When you take a rival’s capital and they still hold land, you choose: absorb them (their cities and army join you, and their troop line can join yours for this game), make them a vassal (they keep their land, fight beside you and pay tribute; vassals count as conquered), or raze the capital for a War Chest and Glory.
  14. Outposts. Barbarian warcamps and the Pretender’s keep are heavily guarded. Clear one and walk your general onto the loot: elite veterans join your band for the rest of the game, and you find a recipe page.
  15. Hidden strongholds. From Crown 1, the six hidden peoples wait somewhere on the map. They never attack, and their fortress is very hard to take. Take it and that people, with its troop line, is yours for good.
  16. Recipes. A union or evolution can be forged the first time only once you hold its full recipe: two pages found in ruins, warcamps and strongholds. After that it is yours in every game.
  17. Decline and Fall. Every empire falls. After a victory you can face it for bonus Glory; if you have not won by 15:00, it begins on its own. Your output drains and your far cities break away one by one (a city holds only while your general stands in it). The peoples you conquered rise behind pretenders and barbarians flood in from every side, until only your capital is left. Protect your capital and your way of life: it ends when your capital falls, and at 20:00 the End of the Age comes for it, and nothing can stop that. You can still win by conquest while your empire unravels. The longer you hold, the more Glory, and the history books judge your reign.
  18. Quests. Every people has three quests in the Collection that pay Glory. Finish all three and that people’s starting troops begin at Lv 2.
